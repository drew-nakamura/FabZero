# Edge AI FPGA customer market

**The strongest prospective niche is engineers already designing FPGA-based edge vision/perception and specialists repeatedly delivering FPGA variants—not edge-AI companies in general.** Start with LogicTronix, Starware Design, Leopard Imaging, Basler and Ouster for technically specific discovery; approach hema as a possible platform partner. Their public material shows relevant engineering work, but none has expressed interest in SiliconeOS. The closest published market segmentation found estimates **edge-AI FPGA hardware at $1.12 billion in 2024, growing to $2.02 billion by 2030 at 10.4% annually**; it does not measure software demand. Prompt-driven FPGA tools already face direct competition from Lattice and Altera, plus mature open-source workflows. A credible opportunity would therefore depend on a narrowly supported task that engineers find easier to verify, reproduce or hand off than their existing process. This report supplies a 14-company research queue and market boundaries, not a customer pipeline or a validated TAM.

Research checked **November 15, 2026**, America/Los_Angeles. Primary company pages, original market publishers, official vendor cases and regulatory filings underpin the findings. All operator/buyer roles, prioritization and commercial fit below are **inferences to test**. No outreach, private budget collection, product trial, quote, payment or new judging round occurred.

## Target existing FPGA engineering work first

The company that operates a camera, robot or edge device is not automatically the company writing its FPGA logic. A deployment may use a GPU, NPU, fixed-function ASIC or an off-the-shelf module. Even an FPGA-containing product can require customer configuration rather than new RTL. The most relevant operator is an FPGA, embedded-imaging or accelerator engineer making repeated design changes; the likely buyer is that engineer's platform or engineering lead. An initial supported comparison might concern parallelism, quantization, buffering, acquisition or preprocessing, with explicit numerical/interface constraints and independent expected results.

Today's SiliconeOS supports signed INT8 matrix multiplication with INT32 accumulation, N=8/16 and one/four lanes in software, with N=8 KV260 physical evidence and an app-to-board-to-signed-app-return workflow. That demonstrates a bounded engineering experiment, not any company workload listed here. Future vision/sensor/network examples require supported IP, board recipes, interfaces, clocks, memory and tests that do not currently exist in this catalog. A conversation can use the present artifact as evidence of the method; a production offer requires an agreed task the product can actually deliver.

For **first technical interviews**, LogicTronix has close KV260/VVAS adjacency; Starware publicly describes release/provenance pain; Leopard builds current FPGA-assisted perception; Basler explicitly invites discussion of easier FPGA programming; Ouster's FPGA hiring lists the exact RTL/verification/toolchain skills. This is a qualitative research order, not estimated purchase likelihood. Basler is also a competing framework developer. Larger firms such as SICK are useful proof that FPGA AI exists, but require substantially more qualification and trust than a first prototype engagement. Public inquiry routes below have not been tested for receptiveness.

## Incumbent prompt tools raise the differentiation bar

On **September16,2026**, Lattice introduced **Lattice Prompt as a free download** for its Radiant ecosystem. Its announcement describes natural-language access to simulation, synthesis, mapping, place-and-route, timing and bitstream generation, through an LLM/agentic IDE and MCP. Its10 ×-or-greater common-task productivity claim is supplier marketing, not an independently verified benchmark or a SiliconeOS result. This is close competition, not merely a general coding assistant. ([Lattice launch](https://ir.latticesemi.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lattice-advances-fpga-design-new-leadership-ai-driven))

Altera's **October21,2026 QuartusIQ announcement** similarly describes contextual natural-language design/debug/validation/timing assistance with MCP, skills and an agent harness. Availability was scheduled for Quartus Prime Pro 26.3 on October28; researchers did not install or independently test it. Altera **expects up to1.5 × faster total design cycles**, illustrating a 24-month project; expectation is not a measured result. Its separate FPGA AI Suite release describes trained-network spatial compilation and a100,000-consecutive-inference early-development license limit, which is an entitlement restriction rather than adoption data. ([QuartusIQ](https://www.altera.com/newsroom/altera-launches-quartusIQ), [FPGA AI Suite 2026.1.1](https://www.altera.com/newsroom/altera-fpga-ai-suite-26-1-1-deterministic-physical-ai-spatial-architecture))

The independent baseline is also capable: Antmicro's Topwrap assembles parameterized HDL, MLE's FPGA-vbs automates variants/build identity, and Basler/Enclustra already pursue higher-level programming/orchestration. Some tool/device/IP combinations cost money, but AMD's current BASIC tier is free with annual renewal and Windows/Linux support. A subscription does not remove tool/IP entitlements. ([AMD licensing](https://www.amd.com/en/products/software/adaptive-socs-and-fpgas/vivado/vivado-licensing-options.html), [FAQ](https://www.amd.com/en/products/software/adaptive-socs-and-fpgas/licensing-faq.html))

The differentiation to test is narrower: **a reliable supported experiment, reviewed constraints, independent checks and inspectable physical-result identity within the engineer's existing workflow**. A paid independent tool must demonstrate incremental value against vendor assistants and existing scripts. Source confidentiality, deterministic failure handling, integration burden and maintenance across tool/board versions may matter more than prompt fluency. Latency and local-data requirements can motivate edge inference, but do not establish that FPGA is preferable to GPU/NPU/ASIC for every task. ([AMD edge-AI architecture discussion](https://www.amd.com/content/dam/amd/en/documents/solutions/automotive/wp-designing-for-the-ai-defined-vehicle.pdf))

## Size software from qualified teams and actual adoption

**No defensible published market size or growth series for prompt-to-FPGA workflow software was found.** There is also no verified worldwide count of custom edge-AI FPGA companies, eligible engineers, FPGA fleets or external software budgets. The 14 names are a discovery sample, not a census. A hardware-revenue share, a camera installed base or a vendor's employee count cannot fill those gaps. Broader market growth supports the existence of relevant engineering activity; it does not prove recurring subscription demand.

At the prior research's **hypothetical $149 per engineer per month**, one continuously paying seat produces **$1,788 in annual revenue**, before churn, discounts and usage charges. An illustrative model is `qualified companies × eligible engineers per company × adoption × $1,788`. **The following inputs are invented scenarios, not observed TAM, SAM or SOM.** If 100 qualified companies each have five eligible engineers and 20% adopt, that gives 100 paying seats and $178,800 in annual recurring revenue (ARR). At 500 companies, five engineers and 20% adoption, the result is 500 seats and $894,000 ARR. At 1,000 companies, ten engineers and 20% adoption, the result is 2,000 seats and $3,576,000 ARR. Independently, 1,000 paying seats would produce $1.788 million ARR. These examples show the arithmetic needed; they do not establish how many customers exist or what they will pay. The earlier $250 assisted evaluation and mature $149 subscription remain unvalidated proposal prices, not active offers.

The next evidence should begin with one actual recent engineering change. Ask an operator to show the present method, what repeats, which evidence a reviewer needs and what is already automated. Observe equivalent supported work using the existing process and SiliconeOS; separate active effort, tool waiting, rework, assistance and reviewer understanding. Then identify the budget owner and test a precise bounded offer. A friendly expert exchange, a referral, a trial and a purchase are different outcomes. Reject or narrow the hypothesis if the useful task exceeds the catalog, existing tools already suffice, setup/checking costs outweigh convenience or there is no repeated use.

Two slide-ready formulations retain the evidence: **“A published 2025 forecast estimates FPGA silicon used in edge AI at about $1.12 billion in 2024, reaching $2.02 billion by 2030 at a 10.4% CAGR. Our software addresses the engineering workflow around a supported subset of those designs.”** And **“Public products and engineering roles at Leopard Imaging, Ouster and SICK demonstrate real FPGA work in perception systems; our next step is testing a specific workflow with those engineers, rather than claiming them as customers.”** A third useful statement is **“Vendor assistants already automate parts of FPGA development; we are testing whether independently checked experiments and clearer handoffs add enough value for a focused team.”** Keep original-source citations on the slides or in notes.

Avoid describing a $100 billion forecast as “our market,” claiming FPGA share is rising in the SHD mix, reporting2026 forecasts as achieved revenue, transferring vendor productivity ratios, or saying this tool replaces engineers/cuts months to minutes. No named company has validated that value. The product opportunity becomes more credible through a narrower task and real switching evidence, even if that yields a smaller market headline.

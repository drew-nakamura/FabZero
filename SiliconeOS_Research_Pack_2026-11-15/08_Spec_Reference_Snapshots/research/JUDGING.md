## Scoring and implications

- **Execution & design — 25 points.** Show an actual running product with clear, usable presentation. For SiliconeOS: observable generated logic on KV260, understandable request/spec/result flow and accurate build status. No results have been demonstrated yet.
- **Real problem, real user — 25 points.** Specific painful task and checkable demand evidence. A public work listing provides context; it does not count as a user who tried SiliconeOS. Highest performance calls for actual use during the event with evidence.
- **Path to customers — 20 points.** Establish buyer, switching reason, price hypothesis and concrete acquisition channel. SiliconeOS proposal: one paid prototype engagement per supported component; neither price nor purchase intent is validated. FPGA-contract marketplaces are a channel hypothesis, not acquired customers.
- **Pitch & demo — 15 points.** Make the customer/problem clear within the opening half-minute and show a coherent live journey; answer limitations directly. Fit the presentation into four minutes, followed by three minutes of questions.
- **Creative use of AI — 15 points.** Show why AI materially enables this workflow. Board-aware interpretation, testable behavior, generated logic and tool-grounded evidence are the proposed story; merely saying AI wrote code is insufficient evidence of differentiation.

Problem, customer acquisition and pitch together account for 60 points; execution and creative AI use account for 40. Technical difficulty and quantity of AI use are not separate scoring criteria. No predicted score is asserted for this unbuilt product.

- Guide permits credited public libraries/templates and research before the event; project-specific demo implementation must be made during the event window. Preserve build history and establish provenance before reusing any existing design. This review has produced planning only.
